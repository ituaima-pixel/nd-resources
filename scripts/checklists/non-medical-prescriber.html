Becoming a non-medical prescriber: step-by-step checklist

For nurses, midwives, pharmacists and allied health professionals in the UK. Tick each step as you go.

1. Before you apply

  • Check your profession can prescribe independently (see the professions table in the guide) and that you are on the NMC, HCPC or GPhC register.
  • Have at least 1 year of post-registration experience in the area you will prescribe in (check your course: some ask for more).
  • Check you have the academic level the course needs (usually Level 6 study for a Level 6 course; a degree or 120 Level 6 credits for Level 7).
  • Decide your scope of practice: the conditions and medicines you will prescribe for, for example ADHD or mental health.
  • Get written support from your line manager or, if self-employed, a clinical setting that will host your learning.
  • Have an enhanced DBS check from the last 3 years, or the DBS update service.

2. Find your supervisors

  • Find a Designated Prescribing Practitioner (DPP) or practice assessor: an experienced prescriber, usually with at least 3 years of prescribing, who meets the DPP competency framework.
  • Find a practice supervisor (NMC courses), who can be the same or a different person.
  • Complete the DPP competency self-assessment together and agree how you will meet the required supervised hours.
  • Arrange the placement or learning-environment audit your university asks for.

3. Funding and application

  • NHS route: ask your trust or practice Non-Medical Prescribing lead, or your local primary care training hub, about a funded place.
  • Self-funded route: compare course fees, start dates and attendance, and budget for fees plus time off work.
  • Get your NMP lead or employer to approve the application, then apply to the university by its deadline.

4. During the course

  • Attend taught days and pass the pharmacology and numeracy exams (numeracy usually needs 100%).
  • Complete your supervised practice hours (often about 90 hours) and keep a log.
  • Build your portfolio against the Competency Framework for all Prescribers.
  • Get your DPP or practice assessor to sign off your competence at the end.

5. After you qualify

  • Your university tells your regulator, and your prescribing qualification is added to the register. Check it appears before you prescribe.
  • Register as a prescriber with your employer and follow its prescribing policy.
  • Write down your scope of practice and keep within it.
  • For private work, get indemnity cover that includes prescribing; for controlled drugs, apply for a private prescriber code if needed.
  • Keep up CPD and audit your prescribing each year.
